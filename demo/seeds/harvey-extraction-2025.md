# Harvey extraction 2025: legal mailbox, past requests and outcomes

**SEED. NOT VERIFIED.** A work tool (Harvey) produced this from a year of old emails. Nobody has checked it against the files, the signed contracts or the contract register. Use it for leads only. Where it disagrees with knowledge/, a matter's corrections or the lawyer, they win and this file is wrong.

## How it was made

- Source: an export of the legal@kestrel-eng.co.uk mailbox and of the Senior Legal Counsel's sent items, 1 January to 31 December 2025, uploaded to a Harvey Vault.
- Run on 12 January 2026. The request was: "List the recurring request types with volumes, what was usually decided, the bank deals, and the typical turnaround."
- Coverage: 1,184 emails in 412 threads. Attachments were read by file name and first pages only; attachments longer than 20 pages were not read in full.
- Counts are the tool's own and approximate. Threads may have been split or merged wrongly.

## 2. What was usually decided

### NDAs (96 threads)

- Paper: the Kestrel template in 37 threads, the other side's paper in 59. The other side's paper was usually accepted after a mark-up.
- Form: 71 mutual, 25 one-way. Most one-way NDAs were customers' early-engagement or tender NDAs.
- Usual changes: term cut to 3 years with 5 for trade secrets (31 threads); governing law changed to English law (22); indemnity deleted (9); non-solicitation of customers deleted (6); residuals clause deleted (3).
- Non-competes: usually deleted (4 threads). We accepted a 2-year non-compete once: a water utility's one-way NDA for early supplier engagement on a treatment works upgrade, signed the same day it arrived (thread "NDA - treatment works early engagement", 3 to 4 March 2025).
- Signatories seen on signed copies: the Chief Financial Officer, the Chief Executive Officer, the Sales Director and the Senior Legal Counsel.

### Customer contracts (54 threads)

- General cap: 100% of the annual fees in 38 of the 47 deals with a cap clause; 150% in 6 deals and 200% in 2, each with the CFO's approval in the thread.
- Data breach: a separate super-cap of 2x the annual fees in 7 deals; 3x in 1 deal (bank deal 1 below); otherwise inside the general cap.
- Unlimited liability: refused in all 9 threads where a customer asked for it, apart from death or personal injury and fraud.
- Indirect loss: excluded for both sides in nearly every deal. In 3 automotive deals, production stoppage costs caused by defective pumps were accepted as direct loss inside the cap.
- Governing law: the customer's law was accepted in 21 of 30 deals with customers outside the UK, mostly German law.
- Time from first draft to signature: 3 to 6 weeks.

### RFQ and tender terms (38 threads)

- An exceptions list went with the bid in 33 threads, usually 4 to 6 exceptions: consequential loss, the overall cap, liquidated damages, IP in deliverables, the warranty period, payment terms.
- Legal's comments went to the bid team a median of 6 working days before the bid deadline.

### Security and supplier questionnaires (31 threads)

- From October 2025, answers were reused from the September 2025 questionnaire after IT Security checked them.
- Questions the earlier answers did not cover went to IT Security.

### Export control and sanctions (22 threads)

- 20 queries ended "no licence required" after a check of the datasheet: standard pumps in cast iron and ductile iron.
- 2 queries were special builds with corrosion-resistant wetted parts and double mechanical seals. Both were treated as controlled under entry 2B350 and export licences were applied for. One licence took 7 weeks to arrive.
- End-user statements were asked for on every shipment outside the UK and the EU.

### Signing authority and powers of attorney (18 threads)

- Mostly questions from Kestrel Pumpen GmbH's finance team. GmbH documents were signed by its two Geschäftsführer together.
- A notary was used twice: a Handelsregister filing for a change of business address, and a share pledge for a bank facility.

### Other kinds

- Data protection: customer DPAs for the monitoring service were agreed with Kestrel's subprocessor list attached.
- Employment: HR forwarded questions; Legal answered them or passed them to outside employment lawyers.
- Disputes: mostly customer warranty claims, settled by credit note or replacement parts.
- Distribution and agency: 2 agency agreements in the Middle East and 1 distributor proposal from Poland, which was declined. No exclusive distributor was appointed in 2025.
- Misrouted: supplier invoices and payment disputes were forwarded to accounts payable in Finance (Sheffield or Dortmund).

## 3. The two bank deals

### Bank deal 1: Brackenford Bank plc (PRA-regulated UK bank)

- What: maintenance of the cooling-water pumps at the bank's two data centres, for 3 years from July 2025. Annual charges about GBP 380,000.
- What the bank asked for: unlimited liability for data breach, uncapped regulatory fines and a general cap of 200%.
- Outcome: general cap 100% of the annual charges; a separate data breach cap of 3x the annual charges, approved by the CFO by email on 18 June 2025, because the bank's outsourcing policy under its regulators' rules required it; regulatory fines inside the 3x cap, not uncapped.
- Source: thread "Brackenford - Schedule 6 liability", 2 May to 30 June 2025, 41 emails.

### Bank deal 2: Wexmoor Building Society

- What: supply and installation of booster pumps with remote condition monitoring at the society's operations centre, signed in October 2025. About GBP 240,000 one-off, plus GBP 18,000 a year for monitoring.
- What the society asked for: a 3x data breach cap and uncapped regulatory fines.
- Outcome: a data breach cap of 2x, approved by Legal; uncapped regulatory fines refused. The society's outsourcing team confirmed the contract was not a material outsourcing, so the 3x exception was not used.
- Source: thread "Wexmoor - liability", 8 September to 21 October 2025, 17 emails.

## 5. Caveats from the tool

- Extracted from email text and attachment names; most attachments were not read.
- Figures and names are as they appear in the emails. They were not checked against signed contracts or the contract register.
- Where a thread ended without a clear outcome, the tool took the last proposal as the outcome.
- This is not legal advice and not a statement of Kestrel's positions.
