# Review table: instructions and columns

## Table-wide instructions (paste into the table's instructions field)

Each file is one email from our legal team's intake mailbox or from a lawyer's own mailbox. Read the whole email, including the earlier messages quoted below it and every attachment. Describe the conversation as far as this email shows it.

Rules for every column:
- Describe what happened. Do not judge it or give legal advice.
- Never guess. If something is not visible, write "not visible".
- Write people as roles, never as names or email addresses ("senior legal counsel", "credit risk analyst", "customer's in-house counsel").
- Write our own group's companies by their full, exact legal names, including the legal form.
- Write outside companies by name.
- No phone numbers, bank details, ID numbers or other personal data.
- Quotes: only our legal team's own wording, 25 words or fewer, in quotation marks.
- If column 1 would say "noise" or "sensitive", answer every other column "skip".
- If this file is an attachment on its own rather than an email (a PST upload may split them out), fill the columns from the document itself, and say in column 2 which email it came with, if that can be seen.
- Plain English, British spelling, short sentences.

## Columns

Add these in order. Use the name exactly as shown. The prompt is what you paste. Free text unless it says otherwise.

**1. Category**

> Classify this email:
> - "work": a request to legal, or legal work, or a reply about one;
> - "noise": newsletter, marketing, auto-reply, system notification, calendar invitation;
> - "attachment": a document on its own, not an email (for example, a draft or a mark-up split out of a PST);
> - "sensitive": an HR or personal matter about an individual (health, conduct, grievance, pay), a colleague's personal legal question, whistleblowing, another party's privileged material, or an email sent to us by mistake.
>
> Answer with the one word only.

**2. Conversation key**

> Give a key that identifies the conversation, so that emails of the same matter can be grouped: our company short name | counterparty short name | the subject without RE:, FW:, AW: or WG: and without any reference number. Example: "Example UK Ltd | Acme | Mutual NDA for data centre projects".

**3. Email date and direction**

> The date of this email (YYYY-MM-DD), and whether it was sent by the legal team ("from legal") or by someone else ("to legal"). Then the date of the earliest message quoted in it.

**4. Source**

> "intake" if it was sent to or from the shared legal intake mailbox; "lawyer's mailbox" otherwise.

**5. Our company**

> The full legal name of our company involved, with its legal form. If the emails give different names or forms for it, list all of them.

**6. Counterparty**

> The outside party: its name; its type (customer, supplier, partner, authority or other); its sector; its size, as far as visible; and the relationship (new, long-standing, strategic) as stated.

**7. Requester**

> Who asked legal: their role and team (for example, "credit risk analyst, new accounts"). Also anyone else in the business who pushed the request.

**8. Work type**

> Choose the one that fits best:
> - NDA review
> - customer contract negotiation (MSA or terms)
> - participation agreement or LPA under a master agreement
> - parent company guarantee or credit security
> - customer onboarding or terms of sale for an entity
> - vendor onboarding or supplier terms
> - customer compliance declarations or forms
> - supplier registration or portal terms
> - contract record lookup
> - security or compliance questionnaire
> - signing authority or entity question
> - dispute or claim
>
> If none fits, write "new:" and a short label. Then add one sentence on what makes it this type.

**9. What was asked**

> In one or two sentences: what the requester wanted from legal, and by when.

**10. Value and urgency**

> The deal, order or claim value if mentioned (with currency), and any deadline or pressure cited (for example, "POs to be secured this week"). "not visible" if none.

**11. What legal did**

> The steps legal took in this conversation, in date order: who (role) did what, and when. Include routing to another lawyer or team. If no legal reply is visible, write "no legal reply visible".

**12. Legal output**

> Classification: advice, mark-up, approval, delegation, question back, refusal, or none visible. Several if several.

**13. Nature of the work**

> Classification:
> - "judgement": needed a lawyer's view;
> - "lookup": the answer was in records or known facts;
> - "process": admin or execution steps;
> - "routing": sent to another team.
>
> Then one sentence why.

**14. Effort**

> Classification: quick (minutes), medium (about an hour), or heavy (more), as it appears from the work shown. Then one sentence why.

**15. Outcome and status**

> What was agreed, approved, sent or decided, as of this email. Then the status: open, done, or unknown.

**16. Turnaround**

> When the request reached legal; when legal first replied; how many chasers; when it finished. Dates or day counts.

**17. Negotiation points**

> For each contract term negotiated in this conversation, one line:
>
> clause topic | their position | our position | their reaction | outcome (accepted ours, agreed fallback, we conceded, they conceded, open, walked away) | final position
>
> Read the attached drafts and mark-ups: tracked changes and comments show both positions. If our position is not visible, write "our position not visible". "none" if there was no negotiation.

**18. Why: context**

> For each negotiation point or decision: why it went that way, as stated or clearly shown. Look for:
> - leverage;
> - customer size or strategic value;
> - deal value;
> - a regulated customer;
> - time pressure;
> - the relationship;
> - precedent;
> - insurance;
> - credit position;
> - who approved.
>
> Write "context not visible" rather than guess.

**19. Our standard positions shown**

> Any position stated or shown as our usual, preferred or standard approach. Examples: "we always prefer to start with our paper"; "waivers should not change". Say who stated it (role).

**20. Company facts**

> Facts about our company revealed in the emails or attachments:
> - entities and their legal forms;
> - who signs or approves what;
> - policies and processes referred to;
> - systems and repositories;
> - certifications and insurance;
> - our contract records with this counterparty (which agreements exist, dates, key terms).
>
> One per line, with the date it was true.

**21. Standard answers given**

> Any standard question legal or the business answered, especially from questionnaires or onboarding forms. One per line: question pattern | our answer | who supplied it.

**22. How we phrase it**

> Up to two short quotes, 25 words or fewer each, of our legal team's own wording that show the tone or a typical phrase. Then one line on the style (length, tone, structure).

**23. Missing documents**

> Documents referred to but not in this file: our replies, mark-ups, the other side's responses, agreements, forms. For each, where it probably is (for example, "commercial counsel's sent items, around 2026-10-09").

**24. Open questions**

> What is unclear or inconsistent and would need the legal team to explain. Examples: an unexplained term or abbreviation, a decision with no visible reason, two different names for the same company.
