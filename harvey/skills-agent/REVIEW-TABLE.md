# Review table: instructions and columns

## Table-wide instructions (paste into the table's instructions field)

Each file is one email from our legal team's intake mailbox or from a lawyer's own mailbox, with its attachments. Read the whole email, including the earlier messages quoted below it and the attachments. Describe the conversation as far as this email shows it.

Rules for every column:
- Describe what happened. Do not judge it or give legal advice.
- Never guess. If something is not visible, write "not visible".
- People as roles, never names or email addresses ("senior legal counsel", "credit risk analyst").
- Our own group's companies by their full, exact legal names. Outside companies by name (the agent codes them later).
- No phone numbers, bank details, ID numbers or other personal data.
- If column 1 says "noise" or "sensitive", answer every other column "skip".
- Plain English, British spelling, short sentences.

## Columns

Add these in order, with the names exactly as shown. Free text unless it says otherwise.

**1. Category**

> Classify this email: "work" (a request to legal, legal work, or a reply about one); "noise" (newsletter, marketing, auto-reply, notification, calendar invitation); "attachment" (a document on its own, not an email); "sensitive" (an HR or personal matter about an individual, whistleblowing, another party's privileged material, or an email sent to us by mistake). Answer with the one word only.

**2. Conversation key**

> A key that groups emails of the same matter: our company short name | counterparty short name | the subject without RE:, FW: or reference numbers.

**3. Date and direction**

> The sent date of this email (YYYY-MM-DD) from its header; "from legal" or "to legal"; and the date of the earliest message quoted in it.

**4. Work type**

> The type of work, in a short label of two to five words (for example "NDA review", "customer contract negotiation", "signing authority question", "security questionnaire"). Reuse the same label for the same kind of work. Then one sentence on what makes it this type.

**5. What was asked**

> Who asked (role and team), what they wanted from legal, and by when.

**6. What legal did and why**

> The steps legal took, in date order, with who (role) did each. For each decision or piece of advice: the reasons given or clearly shown, such as the sum at stake against the cost of acting, the relationship, leverage, deal value, time pressure, precedent, risk, or who approved. "reasons not visible" rather than a guess.

**7. Positions and outcome**

> For each point negotiated or decided: their position | our position | what happened | why. Read the attached drafts and mark-ups: tracked changes and comments show both sides. "none" if nothing was negotiated.

**8. Stated as firm**

> Anything someone stated as how we always or never work, a red line, or an approval limit: the statement and who said it (role). "none" if nothing.

**9. Knowledge used or needed**

> The facts legal had to know or look up to do this: for example which group company contracts, who may sign, insurance or certifications, an existing contract with this counterparty, a policy, a template. For each: the kind of fact, the fact itself if shown (with the date it was true), and where it came from (a system, a team, a document, memory).

**10. How we phrase it**

> Up to two quotes of our legal team's own wording, 25 words or fewer each. Then one line on style.

**11. Open questions**

> What is unclear or inconsistent and would need the legal team to explain: an unexplained term, a decision with no visible reason, two names for one company.
